When browsing developer portfolios, you almost always see the same pattern: immaculate hero images, flawless live demos, and a laundry list of modern technologies. Everything looks effortless.

Yet anyone who has actually built software knows the real truth: **90% of genuine engineering happens in the moments where you have no clue why something is failing.**

## The Problem with Traditional Showcases

When we only showcase the final shiny artifact, we strip away all the most valuable insights:
- Which initial architectural assumptions were completely wrong?
- Which subtle edge cases or OS abstractions caused days of confusion?
- What mental model shift was required to unlock the real solution?

Sharing only the end product creates an illusion that clean code springs fully formed from competent developers. It also means you lose a personal archive of hard-won debugging reflexes.

## The Learning Curve Ledger

This site includes a dedicated **Learning Curves** section for this exact reason. For every challenging project or system obstacle, I document three things:

1. **The Wall:** The initial roadblock, false hypotheses, and why intuitive fixes failed.
2. **The Turning Point:** The specific RFC, source code inspection, or mental model breakthrough.
3. **The Durable Rule:** An actionable heuristic I can carry forward into future projects.

## Benefits in Practice

Documenting what went wrong forces you to understand *why* the fix worked rather than celebrating that the tests turned green and moving on.

If you are an engineer or creator building tools, I encourage you to document your learning curves too. The community has enough tutorials on the basics—what we truly need are honest field notes from the trenches.
